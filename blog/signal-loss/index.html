Why good tools still leave gaps between teams

Stuart White Updated September 22, 2026 3 min read

A team can have capable people, useful software, and documented processes, yet still lose important information between one step and the next. Decisions stay in messages. Requirements get copied by hand. The next person starts work without knowing what was agreed.

I call that signal loss: information exists, but it does not reach the person who needs it with enough context to act.

Replacing a tool may help. Before doing that, I want to understand the route the information takes and the effort required to keep it moving.

Look at the path people use

A quick message can be easier than opening a project, finding the right record, and completing several fields. If that is how work gets done, the workaround tells you something about the formal process.

The useful questions are specific. What makes the informal path easier? Which information is missing when it reaches the next team? What does the sender need to know to make a useful handoff?

A workaround is a useful place to start looking for friction.

Training and clearer expectations can help, but they work better when the process itself is practical. Asking people to record the same information twice leaves the extra work in place.

Three places to look

Handoffs. Information changes shape as it moves between teams. A short summary may omit the reason for a decision, a customer constraint, or an assumption the next person needs to check.

Meetings. A discussion produces decisions and actions, but the notes do not reach the people expected to act. Later, participants remember different versions of the agreement.

Individual knowledge. Someone knows why a process works the way it does, but there is no convenient place to record that context. A vacation or role change exposes the dependency.

Make the handoff easier

Consider an illustrative sales-to-delivery workflow. A deal closes in a CRM. Sales emails the details to operations, which copies them into a project tool. Requirements can be lost during that re-entry.

One possible improvement is to create a draft delivery record from the CRM when the deal closes. The teams agree on the required fields, identify the owner, and review missing information before delivery starts.

The automation handles the copying. People still need to check the requirements, resolve uncertainty, and accept the handoff. Moving incomplete information faster does not make it complete.

I would check the change with both teams. Does it reduce re-entry? Are fewer clarifications needed? Can delivery find the original context? Those questions help establish whether the new workflow is useful.

Make the information easier to pass on, and make responsibility for the next step clear.

The same approach applies to meeting notes, support escalations, and recurring reports. Follow the information, find the repeated effort, and make a focused change that the people involved can use.
