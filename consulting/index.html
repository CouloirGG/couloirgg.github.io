// the method

From discovery to a working fix.

I use these five steps to understand the work, agree priorities, and make useful changes. The scope and pace depend on your team and the problem.

01
Understand the business
I review the business, its products, and its operating context before we meet. That gives us a starting point for the questions, without assuming I already know the answer.
Observe
02
Agree what needs to change
I start with the people responsible for the outcome. We discuss what feels difficult, what has been tried, and how we will recognise an improvement.
Observe
03
Talk to the people doing the work
I talk with the people handling the work each day. Their handoffs and workarounds help explain where the process fits and where it creates extra effort.
Analyse
04
Map the problem
I bring the workflow, responsibilities, and dependencies into one picture. We identify where context gets lost, how the problem affects other teams, and which changes are worth making.
Analyse
05
Build and validate the change
The agreed changes might be an automation, a clearer handoff, or a small internal tool. I build and document the change, then check it with the people who will use it.
Execute

// daily friction

Make room for
the work that matters.

Repeated administrative work adds up. Removing a small task from several workflows can give a team more time for the work it is there to do.

Start with repeated work. Look for information copied between tools, updates assembled by hand, and the same clarification requested at every handoff.
Keep what already works. Make a focused change around the parts people rely on, and check that it reduces effort in practice.
Check the effect across the team. A change that saves one person time should not leave someone else with more work. Follow it through the full workflow.
Possible changes: automate a recurring report, clarify who owns a handoff, or put a decision where the next team can find it.
